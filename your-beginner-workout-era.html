How to start exercising, find movement you actually love, and build a routine that fits your life. 💗

Three months until the new year. Three months to start showing up for yourself. ✨

So, you have about three months until the new year, and you're ready to get into the best shape of your life. But where do you even start?

Maybe you're confused about which workout plan is best for you. Maybe you've tried a few routines but nothing seems to stick, or you're wondering whether your diet is holding you back. Girl, I get it. There's so much fitness advice online.

Fitness isn't one-size-fits-all. Building muscle while reducing body fat may call for different training and nutrition than improving endurance or flexibility. Your experience, schedule, energy and preferences all matter. Today we're keeping things simple with four beginner-friendly ways to move.

First, a little nutrition talk 🥑

Exercise is only one part of your wellness journey. Nutrition, sleep, recovery and consistency matter, too. Weight loss generally requires a calorie deficit over time, while weight gain generally requires consuming more energy than you use. Building muscle also calls for adequate nutrition, including protein, and progressive strength training.

But you don't need to obsess over calories to begin taking care of yourself. Focus on nourishing meals, hydration and habits you can maintain. Individual needs vary.

Step 1: Find your kind of movement 🌸

What kind of movement actually makes you feel good? Maybe it's lifting weights, a heated Pilates class, tennis, running, cycling or barre. Personally, I love classes because I can try something new without planning every exercise myself.

You don't have to force yourself to become a runner if you hate running. The best workout routine is one you enjoy enough to keep doing.

Step 2: Listen to your body throughout the month 🌷

Energy and comfort can change throughout the menstrual cycle. Here are four commonly described phases and some movement ideas:

🌷

Menstrual Phase

Rest & recharge

Gentle walks, stretching, restorative yoga or light Pilates can feel good when energy is low.

🧘🏻‍♀️
🌿

Follicular Phase

Build momentum

If you feel more energized, try strength training, Pilates, cycling, running or a new class.

🏋🏻‍♀️
☀️

Ovulatory Phase

Move your way

Feeling strong? Try tennis, a challenging gym session, cycling or intervals if you enjoy them.

🏃🏻‍♀️
🎀

Luteal Phase

Support your energy

Moderate lifting, barre, Pilates, walking or extra recovery can be lovely choices.

🧘🏽‍♀️

These are options, not rules. Research does not support one universal workout schedule based on menstrual phases. Let your symptoms, comfort and energy guide you. Saunas and ice baths are optional, not necessary for fitness results.

Plan 01 · 45 minutes

The Cozy At-Home Pilates Girl 🧘‍♀️

For low-impact movement, core work and a relaxing workout atmosphere.

30-minute mat Pilates

Warm-up · 5 minutes: Cat-cow (1 min), pelvic tilts (1 min), spinal twists (1 min), glute bridges (2 min).

Main workout · 20 minutes: Complete two rounds. Work for about 45 seconds, then rest or transition for 15 seconds.

  1. Glute bridges
  2. Dead bugs
  3. Side-lying leg lifts — right
  4. Side-lying leg lifts — left
  5. Bird dogs
  6. Clamshells — right
  7. Clamshells — left
  8. Heel taps
  9. Modified side plank — right
  10. Modified side plank — left

Cool-down · 5 minutes: Child's pose, seated forward fold and gentle hip stretches.

Then stretch for 15 minutes

Spend a few minutes on your hamstrings, hip flexors, glutes, shoulders and back. Try Pilates two or three times a week to start. You only need a mat, water bottle and comfortable clothes; a mini band is optional.

Plan 03 · 35–45 minutes

The Beginner Running Girl 👟

For building endurance and working toward your first 5K.

Warm-up · 5 minutes: Walk at an easy pace, gradually increasing speed.

Main workout · 24 minutes: Repeat eight times: jog gently for 1 minute, then walk for 2 minutes. Keep a pace that lets you speak in short sentences.

Cool-down · 5 minutes: Walk slowly, then stretch calves, quads, hamstrings and hip flexors for another 5–10 minutes.

A few things every beginner should know 💌

Rest counts. You don't have to work out every day. Consistency beats intensity. A manageable routine is better than an exhausting one you can't maintain. Progress is more than a number. Notice strength, energy, confidence, mood and sleep. And you can always change your mind about which workouts you love.
