Surviving College as a Broke Student

Realistic ways to save money, use the resources around you, and make college life a little easier without spending a fortune.

Being in college can be difficult and stressful. Whether you're an international student, an out-of-state student, or attending school close to home, the financial side of college can be a lot to handle.

Between tuition, textbooks, groceries, transportation, housing, and everyday expenses, it can feel like your money disappears almost as quickly as you earn it. Many students are also working part-time while trying to save money and stay focused on school.

If that sounds familiar, here are a few things that helped me and may help make college life a little more affordable for you too. 🍊

Tip #1

Check Your School's Food Pantry

Check your school's website or student services department to see whether your college has a food pantry. Many universities have food pantries available to students, and they can be an incredibly helpful resource.

Depending on your school, you may find pantry staples, snacks, canned foods, toiletries, household products, and other everyday essentials. Using these resources can help reduce your grocery and household expenses throughout the semester.

Student resources exist for students to use. Don't be afraid to find out what's available on your campus.

Tip #2

Be Strategic About Groceries

If you're trying to eat well while staying on a budget, stores such as Aldi or Lidl can be helpful places to shop for affordable groceries, including fresh fruits and vegetables.

You can also make fresh produce last longer by storing it properly. Depending on the vegetable, using paper towels to absorb excess moisture or freezing produce before it spoils can help reduce food waste.

Frozen vegetables and canned foods are also worth keeping around. They're convenient, usually last much longer than fresh food, and can make it easier to put together affordable meals when you're busy with classes.

Tip #3

Use Your Student Email for Discounts

Please use that college email address! Your student email can unlock discounts and special student pricing that you might otherwise miss.

Student discounts may be available for things such as entertainment, music subscriptions, technology, transportation, museums, software, memberships, and other services.

Before paying full price for something, take a minute to search whether the company offers student pricing. Those small savings can really add up over the course of a semester.

Tip #4

Get a Local Library Card

Your local public library can offer much more than books. A library card may give you access to ebooks, textbooks, study spaces, online resources, technology, and other services for free.

During my undergraduate years, I was even able to check out a Wi-Fi hotspot from my local library. Having access to free internet helped me save money on my phone and internet expenses.

Not every library offers the same services, but that's exactly why it's worth checking. You may discover resources you didn't even know were available.

Tip #5

Build Your College Community

Get out and make friends. Having a strong community during college is important for so many reasons, especially when you're trying to figure out adulthood and finances at the same time.

The people around you may have experiences, resources, and advice that can help you. Friends can share information about campus programs, affordable places to shop, free events, transportation options, and other opportunities you might not have discovered on your own.

Depending on your situation, trusted friends may also be able to coordinate rides, cook meals together, or split certain shared expenses. College doesn't have to be something you navigate completely by yourself.

You Don't Need to Have College Completely Figured Out 🌸

Being a student on a tight budget can be challenging, but learning how to use the resources around you can make a real difference. Start with your campus, your library, your student discounts, and your community. Sometimes the best money-saving resources are already available — you just have to know where to look.
