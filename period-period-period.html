Period. Period. Period.

Because sometimes being a woman means showing up for a world that doesn't always make room for what your body is going through.

As women, we go through a lot.

We live in a society that often tells us to be soft, feminine, graceful, and nurturing but at the same time expects us to work, study, produce, and perform as though our bodies never change.

Wake up.
Go to class.
Go to work.
Meet the deadline.
Finish the assignment.
Take care of everyone else.
Repeat.

And somewhere in the middle of all of that, your period comes.

Maybe you're tired.

Maybe you're emotional.

Maybe you're bloated and uncomfortable.

Or maybe you're experiencing cramps so painful that getting out of bed feels like an accomplishment on its own.

And yet, life continues as if nothing happened.

“I need one or two days.”

The other day, I was studying with a friend in the graduate student lounge when our conversation turned to periods.

She told me that she experiences severe menstrual cramps and sometimes needs one or two days each month to recover.

But trying to plan school around those days has been difficult.

She explained that when she tried to communicate her needs to her professors, she was told that she could only miss a limited amount of class and that illness-related absences might require documentation from a doctor.

We looked at each other.

And we laughed.

Not because anything about the situation was funny.

It was the kind of laugh that happens when another woman tells you something and you understand exactly what she means without her having to explain much more.

That little: “Girl... I know.”

Because when you've experienced debilitating cramps or watched someone you love experience them you know that sometimes it isn't simply a matter of “pushing through.”

Sometimes your body is asking you to stop.

But what if getting help isn't that simple?

This is something I don't think we talk about enough.

Imagine waking up in intense pain while living alone.

Maybe you're a student. Maybe your family lives far away. Maybe you don't have someone who can immediately drive you somewhere. Maybe you're in too much pain to comfortably drive yourself.

And depending on where you live and your circumstances, even seeking urgent medical care can come with practical and financial concerns.

So sometimes women stay home.

We curl up in bed. We grab the heating pad. We take whatever measures normally help us.

And somehow, while dealing with all of that, we're still thinking:

I have an assignment due.
I'm supposed to work today.
What am I going to tell my professor?
Am I going to fall behind?

That's the part that gets me.

“Your body can be screaming at you to rest while the rest of your life is screaming at you to keep going.”

Your body isn't an inconvenience 🌸

I don't think menstruation should be treated like something embarrassing that women are simply expected to quietly manage behind the scenes.

For one person, a period might mean a few uncomfortable days. For another, menstrual symptoms can affect school, work, exercise, sleep, social plans, and everyday life.

And sometimes, the hardest part isn't even the pain itself.

It's knowing that everything around you is still moving.

The assignment is still due. Your shift still starts at the same time. Your professor still expects you in class. Your responsibilities are still waiting for you.

Needing rest doesn't make someone lazy. Struggling doesn't make someone weak. And being in pain shouldn't automatically mean you have to suffer silently just to prove that you can keep up.

I keep wondering what would happen if we listened more

What would our schools look like if conversations about menstrual health were taken more seriously?

What would workplaces look like?

What would healthcare look like?

What would research look like?

I don't pretend to have all the answers.

But I think change can begin with something incredibly simple:

“This is happening to me.”

And instead of immediately questioning, minimizing, or dismissing her experience, someone listens.

That's one of the reasons I wanted to write this post.

Not because I have the perfect solution.

But because I know my friend isn't the only woman who has sat somewhere wondering:

How am I supposed to keep doing everything when my body is asking me to stop?
